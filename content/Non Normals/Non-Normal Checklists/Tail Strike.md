## Zusatzinformationen

> [!info]- Erkennung
> Tail strike occurs when the lower aft fuselage contacts the runway during takeoff or landing. A significant factor is the **lack of flight crew experience** in the model being flown.
>
> Can be identified by:
> - A noticeable bump or jolt
> - A scraping noise from the tail
> - TAILSKID or TAIL STRIKE EICAS message or light
>
> **Note:** Anytime fuselage contact is suspected or confirmed, accomplish the appropriate NNC without delay.

> [!warning]- Takeoff Risk Factors
> **Mistrimmed Stabilizer** — Usually results from erroneous takeoff data (wrong weights, incorrect CG) or incorrect FMS/stabilizer setting. Challenge the reasonableness of load sheet numbers.
>
> **Rotation at Improper Speed** — Early rotation or rotation at too-low airspeed for the weight/flap setting.
>
> **Trimming during Rotation** — PF may lose elevator feel while trim is running → excessive rotation rate.
>
> **Excessive Rotation Rate** — Especially when transitioning from unpowered to hydraulically assisted flight controls. Control input required for proper rotation rate varies between models.
>
> **Improper Use of Flight Director** — FD provides accurate pitch guidance only **after** the airplane is airborne. With proper rotation rate, airplane reaches 35 ft at ~15° pitch. Aggressive rotation into the pitch bar at takeoff is not appropriate.

> [!warning]- Landing Risk Factors
> **Unstabilized Approach** — Biggest single cause of landing tail strikes. Stabilize all variables by **1,000 ft AFE (IMC)** / **500 ft AFE (VMC)**. If unstabilized at these gates, consider go-around.
> At touchdown, ground spoilers add nose-up pitch force, reducing pitch authority — a firm touchdown on the main gear is often preferable to a soft one with the nose rising rapidly.
>
> **Holding Off in the Flare** — Extended flare with airspeed loss results in a dropped-in touchdown. A smooth/soft landing is not essential, especially on a wet runway.
>
> **Trimming in the Flare** — Trim during the approach, **not in the flare**. Too much trim raises the nose and can cause a balloon, followed by a dropped-in or three-point landing.
>
> **Mishandling of Crosswinds** — Sideslip reduces lift, increases drag, increases sink rate. Shifting winds below 100 ft make flare timing difficult. Use additional thrust if needed; maintain stable descent rate to flare initiation.
>
> **Over-Rotation during Go-Around** — Late go-arounds (during flare or after touchdown) are a common cause. Upon go-around mode engagement, FD immediately commands go-around pitch attitude. Abrupt rotation into the pitch bar before the airplane responds → tail strike. A **brief gear touchdown during a late go-around is acceptable** — do not rotate aggressively to avoid it.
